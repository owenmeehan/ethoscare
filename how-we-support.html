The work

Practical help, a weekly key-work session, and a plan with the young person’s name on it.

Staff take care of practical, emotional and religious needs, and step back as a young person can do more alone. Advocacy is there when it is needed. Independence is the point.

What support includes

  • An individual pathway plan covering education, employment, health, relationships, personal goals, culture and religion.
  • A named key worker and agreed one-to-one hours each week, planned or at short notice when something cannot wait.
  • Help to register with a GP and dentist, attend appointments, and manage medication.
  • Links to CAMHS or other mental health services, and to drug and alcohol services where that is relevant.
  • Support with benefits, budgets, cooking, healthy eating and the skills needed for a future tenancy.
  • Help to find leisure and hobbies, and to use what Southsea already offers.
  • Interpreters, culturally appropriate support, ESOL and help reaching legal advice for unaccompanied young people.
  • Crisis support and calm de-escalation when a day goes badly.
  • Weekly group time, including cooking together. Residents choose the meal, across cultures and faiths.
How the day is staffed

The phone is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year. The staff office at the front of the house is also the sleep-in room, so a member of staff is in the home overnight.

Working with the placing authority

Support hours, staff roles and the arrangements around a placement are set out clearly so the local authority can make a proper decision and stay involved. Pathway plans are written with the young person and shared with the professionals around them.

Policies, the statement of purpose and registration documents are available to placing authorities on request. This website does not publish an inspection grade.
