Placements

Young people ready for a supported home, not a children’s home.

Each house can take up to six young people, aged 16 to 24, in a mixed-gender household. The group is planned around safety, goals and who already lives there.

Who the homes are for

Montgomerie House and Hudson House are for care leavers and unaccompanied asylum-seeking young people who are building independence. They are for young people who no longer need the level of care given in foster care or a children’s home, and who still need structure, guidance and a stable address.

A placement is offered only after a referral assessment, including risk and compatibility. A new resident has to be a safe match for the people already in the house. Needs that cannot be held safely alongside the current group will be declined.

Needs we may be able to support

Where the assessment shows it is safe for that young person and for the household, support may include:

  • Autism
  • ADHD
  • Attachment difficulties
  • Mild to moderate mental health needs
  • Social, emotional and mental health needs
  • Moderate learning disabilities
  • Low-level substance misuse
  • Self-harm
  • Risk-taking behaviour
  • A history of gang association or criminal exploitation
  • Probation or youth offending interventions

Unaccompanied young people

Local authorities also place unaccompanied asylum-seeking young people aged 16 to 24. Many have lived through trauma, displacement or exploitation. Support can include interpreters, help to reach legal advice, ESOL, trauma-informed practice, and attention to culture, language and faith. The same compatibility assessment applies before any offer.
